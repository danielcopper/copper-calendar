- D27 (changed 2026-11-11): On each update the month and "Woche" bring today's row flush to the top of their list: the month's current week, "Woche"'s current day; once the app fetches from Home Assistant (D3), an update is also each fetch and midnight. "Woche quer" is not covered: its list holds one row, and its days are columns. A widget's views offer no scroll that puts a row at the top, only a smooth scroll to a row (`setScrollPosition`; `setRelativeScrollPosition` ends in the same), which AOSP's `AbsListView.PositionScroller` ends by these rules. A row below the shown ones (`MOVE_DOWN_POS`) ends with its bottom at the list's bottom padding or 12 dp above the list's bottom (the fading edge length, its `mExtraScroll`), whichever is more; the list's last row at the padding alone. A row already shown (`scrollToVisible`) moves only as far as it has to: with its top above the list's top, until its top is at the top; else, with its bottom below the padding, until its bottom is at the padding; else not at all. So the list scrolls to the last row whose rows from today's on fit into it with those 12 dp to spare (the list's last row needs none), its bottom padding is the list's height less those rows, and it does not clip its rows to its padding (`clipToPadding` false), so the rows after that one still show. The list's height is the widget's greatest height as the launcher reports it (`appWidgetMaxHeight`, its height in portrait), less the card's border and padding and the header. In landscape, or wherever else the launcher shows the widget at another height than its greatest (in landscape its least), the padding is too large by the difference and today can end above the top; this is not handled. When the launcher reports no greatest height, the list scrolls today's row only into view, without padding, and the log warns: a default of 4 cells (456 dp) would make the padding too large on a lower widget and could scroll today out of view above the top (owner, 2026-11-11). When the rows from today to the end fit into the list, it scrolls to its end without padding, and the rows before today show above them, the topmost possibly cut. When today's row alone is taller than the list less those 12 dp, it gets no padding. A scroll to it from rows above ends with its bottom 12 dp above the list's bottom, or at the bottom when it is the list's last row or was shown with its top in the list, and its top cut when it is taller than the room above that end. An update that then finds it shown with its top cut brings its top to the list's top: a row taller than the list shows its top and its bottom by turns from update to update, one no taller stays flush on top from then on. A last row no taller than the list falls under the rows to the end that fit. The padding stays when the user scrolls to the end: blank room below the last row. An update that finds the list scrolled further down than that row can leave today above the top: the scroller then moves up only until the row shows, or not at all when it already does. A widget built anew, added to the home screen or after the app is installed or updated, shows today on top only from its next update: its update comes before its list has rows, and the scroll then does nothing (`PositionScroller.start` returns while the list has no children). A second update a second or two after a new widget was the alternative and was dropped (owner, 2026-11-11): it is more code, and its delay would be a guess. Measured on the phone (2026-11-11): today stands flush on top after a resize and after the periodic update every 30 minutes, also with the phone locked. The setup app makes this a setting per widget, "Zurück zu heute": on each update (the default), only at midnight, never, or every 15, 30 or 60 minutes. Shorter intervals were dropped: WorkManager runs periodic work no more often than every 15 minutes, and shorter alarms are inexact and deferred while the phone dozes unless they are exact, which needs a permission the user grants by hand.
- D28 (2026-11-10): Gradle checks every dependency the build resolves, plugins included, against SHA-256 checksums in `gradle/verification-metadata.xml`, and locks the resolved versions of the app's configurations and of the build script's classpath in lock files; Robolectric's own download of its Android jars at test time is outside both; a dependency change rewrites both with `mise run deps-refresh`, and the diff is read before the commit. This answers SonarQube Cloud's findings that dependencies are neither verified nor locked; accepting them in SonarQube Cloud was the alternative and was dropped, since the checks cost one command per update. Checksums only, no PGP signatures: an update rewrites the lock files in any case, and signatures would add a key ring to keep and a key server to reach on each rewrite, while 55 artifacts would still need checksums, 33 of them unsigned (among them the ktlint plugin and the SonarQube plugin's marker) and 22 signed with one of six keys no key server had (2026-11-10).
- D29 (2026-11-10): Dependabot does not rewrite the checksums (it has no support for Gradle's verification metadata, dependabot-core issue 1996, open since 2020); of the lock files it rewrites only `gradle.lockfile`, by running Gradle, which then fails on the missing checksum, and `buildscript-gradle.lockfile`, where the plugins are locked, it never touches. So its Gradle pull requests stay red until a person runs `mise run deps-refresh` on the branch, reads the diff and pushes it; a CI check fails a pull request whose lock files or checksums no longer match what its dependencies resolve to and names that task. Checksums a removed dependency leaves behind do not fail it; `mise run deps-refresh` drops them. A workflow that rewrites both and commits them with a GitHub App's or a personal access token, so the checks run again on its commit, was the alternative and was dropped: for a repository with one maintainer it is a secret with write access to set up and keep, and new checksums would land without anyone reading them.
- D30 (2026-11-10): A tap on a Home Assistant target starts two things, one after the other: first the app that handles the link, started as its icon starts it (its launch intent from the package manager, `ACTION_MAIN` with `CATEGORY_LAUNCHER`), with `FLAG_ACTIVITY_NEW_TASK | FLAG_ACTIVITY_CLEAR_TASK`; then the link itself, without flags. Back from the target then returns to the page the Home Assistant app itself opens with. The app is found by the link's scheme, not by a package name, since Home Assistant ships more than one build (a full and a minimal one); this app declares that it looks for the apps that open `homeassistant://navigate` links (`<queries>`), which Android 11 and later require before they show another app. The query names the host too: Android matches a query against the other app's filters, a query without a host matches no filter that names one, and the Home Assistant app's filter names `navigate`; the first build of this variant had no host, saw no app and opened every link alone (2026-11-10). When no app is seen to handle the link, when several do and none is the default, or when the one that does has no launcher entry, the link opens alone in a new task, without clearing, and the log says which; with no app at all it then fails as before (D25). A target in another app opens alone, in a new task. Without this, each tap stacked one more calendar page in the Home Assistant app's task, so back stepped through every earlier tap: its `LinkActivity` (`singleTask`) starts a new `LaunchActivity` (standard launch mode, no flags) for every `homeassistant://navigate/…` link (read in home-assistant/android at `c6eb57c`). Two variants failed on the phone (2026-11-10). Clearing the task and opening the target alone left one page, but back closed the Home Assistant app. Opening the dashboard's start page link first and the target link after it worked from a shell, but from the widget the two links reached the system 4 to 7 ms apart, both to the same `singleTask` `LinkActivity`, and the system's log showed one `LinkActivity` displayed and one `LaunchActivity` started: `LinkActivity` ignores a further link it receives while it is still open. The launcher start goes to another activity than the link, so the link cannot land in an open `LinkActivity` that way. Measured on the phone (2026-11-10): started back to back with `adb`, the calendar opens on the tapped day, the task holds two `LaunchActivity` records, and back returns to the Home Assistant app's own start screen. From the widget, nine taps with the Home Assistant app running each started it as its icon does, with `FLAG_ACTIVITY_NEW_TASK | FLAG_ACTIVITY_CLEAR_TASK`, and then the link, with no fallback logged and no background start blocked; the task held exactly two `LaunchActivity` records, and back returned to the app's start page. With the app swiped away from recents, a tap works too and takes longer. The system lists the Home Assistant app among the apps this one may see (`dumpsys package queries`). The cost: whatever the user had open in the Home Assistant app is gone after a tap, and the app loads two pages per tap. The opening animation ran twice in the shell test of two page links; for the launcher start it was not looked at. Waiting for a fix in the Home Assistant app was dropped: issue home-assistant/android#5381 is open, and pull request #7163 covers only notification commands. Once that app opens navigate links in place, a tap goes back to opening its target alone, without clearing.
